\begin{abstract}
Sparse attention must retain useful candidates at low selection cost. We study LoDA-SA, a distance-aware two-level selector: a near--far page-to-token cascade with shared 32-coordinate low-frequency RoPE scoring, original-dimensional attention on selected tokens, and absolute budgets: protected sink and window lengths, near extent $n_l$, near page budget $n_b$ with far pages $K_1-n_b$ by subtraction, and one free-competition token pool; nonaligned protection-boundary pages join candidates outside the quotas. Ratio-coupled parameterizations instead silently reallocate far capacity: one expression links the far token budget to three knobs, and archived diagnostics exhibit all three failures, including an exact integer threshold. The absolute interface makes page capacities explicit and drops regional token quotas. An archived five-task LongBench summary reports 86 configurations and one control: quota-free selection at 32 near pages and 4096-token extent scores 48.26 versus 47.46 for the control, while a numeric-token-quota arm at the same geometry scores 48.67. These descriptive results do not isolate either regional axis or establish that removing a token quota preserves quality; archived RULER 32K runs reach 59.29 under different configurations. These single-run comparisons establish neither non-inferiority nor mechanisms; historical timings show slower sparse decoding, and the compact 4/128 design still requires matched quality and preparation-inclusive complete-request cost.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Long-context decoding must retain access to useful history under a finite attention budget. Tokens at different distances compete for this budget, and global ranking alone does not reserve candidate access for a positional region. Distance therefore offers a way to organize selection, without assuming that importance decreases monotonically with distance or that near tokens are always preferable. A selector must decide which regions can supply candidates and then rank those candidates cheaply enough to justify restricting final attention.

Cascades such as AsyncTLS shortlist pages using inexpensive summaries before token refinement~\cite{asynctls}. Mean pooling and sign-aware extrema, summaries our selector also uses, do not rank pages by their total attention weight. Following ClusterKV's token/page perspective~\cite{clusterkv}, Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(a) shows a single Qwen3 query where fewer missed reference pages coincide with more missed reference mass. Unequal regional allocations and realized counts preclude a controlled comparison but motivate examining how scoring and budgets shape candidate access.

Across 128 correlated observations from four sequences, the two global selectors have different regional missed-mass profiles (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}(b)). Mean missed mass, normalized by each query's total mass-ranked reference mass, is .084 near and .170 far for block averaging, versus .079 and .042 for sign-aware extrema. Extrema have lower mean misses in both regions, establishing neither regional specialization nor near-region equivalence. With a fixed 2048-token cap, the best of 23 tested near-avg/far-extrema splits has mean missed mass .135, versus .121 for global extrema. This bounded diagnostic scan establishes neither a hybrid advantage nor a task-quality benefit.

We study a two-level selector that couples a shared scoring view with absolute regional budgets. Both stages use a fixed set of 32 coordinates comprising 16 complete low-frequency RoPE pairs; the near page budget $n_b$ and near extent $n_l$ are absolute, far pages follow by subtraction, nonaligned protection-boundary pages join candidates outside the quotas, and L2 refines tokens in a single free-competition pool. Among the three 32-coordinate subsets in Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(c), low-frequency pairs retain .84 of full-dimensional reference mid-TopK mass on average, random coordinates .51, high-frequency pairs .24; the random subset does not preserve RoPE pairs. Selected tokens use original-dimensional Q/K/V in one final attention operation. Compact coordinates narrow each scoring dot product, but ordinary L2 still scores the full history before masking; fewer shortlisted pages do not remove that work. We also formulate a prefill-informed far gate motivated by layer variation in Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(d); it is disabled by default.

We contribute LoDA-SA, an absolute-budget two-level selector with shared low-frequency coordinates, a unified analysis of implicit budget coupling in ratio parameterizations, a descriptive grid comparison that distinguishes regional geometry from token-allocation choices, and separate evaluations of task quality, regional coverage and execution cost. Under the archived ratio interface, one expression in the page cap, near share and fill fraction determines the far token budget, so raising any of the three can zero it; reported Qwen3-8B RULER 32K AVG moves 0.99 points when archived near quotas shift (16,640) to (8,320), with both quotas changing together. Grid cells are single runs, and the archived sparse configuration decodes slower than dense at every measured length. Establishing a quality--latency benefit for the compact design requires matched quality and preparation-inclusive complete-request measurements.

\section{Related Work}
Quest supplies sign-aware page-extremum bounds, SparQ selects key coordinates from queries, and HISA prunes the attention set hierarchically~\cite{quest,sparq,hisa}. AsyncTLS explicitly follows Quest for coarse block selection and applies Double Sparsity for fine token selection; its cascade also includes quantization, per-query normalization with GQA probability aggregation and original-KV attention~\cite{asynctls}. Earlier Twilight uses a select-then-prune architecture~\cite{twilight}; LoDA-SA studies explicit distance-region page access within this hierarchical-selection paradigm, with complete low-frequency RoPE pairs shared across both stages and unified L2 competition. The relationship is algorithmic; code lineage is unverified.

The selector's own reads also matter. Token-Picker uses conservative probability estimates from partial key-bit chunks to stop further key-chunk and value transfers for pruned tokens~\cite{tokenpicker}; our selector instead ranks candidates with a fixed coordinate subset under page budgets, keeping original-dimensional KV. Our selector keeps both selection stages in bf16: AsyncTLS token keys are INT4, FFD scans 2-bit keys, and official HISA pools block summaries in FP8~\cite{asynctls,ffd,hisa}; LoDA-SA targets lower selection overhead through compact scoring and structured candidate admission; ordinary L2 still scores the full history, so page-level narrowing alone does not establish that saving. KV compression and placement methods address complementary storage and movement constraints~\cite{shadowkv,million,clusterkv}; a smaller scoring view retains original-KV capacity and does not by itself establish lower complete-request latency.

\section{LoDA-SA: Distance-Aware Selection in a Shared Subspace}
LoDA-SA separates three decisions: which distance regions can admit pages, how to score them with a shared compact view, and which pooled tokens enter final attention. The distance policy uses $n_l$ for near extent and $n_b$ for its page budget, leaving $K_1-n_b$ far pages. The implementation prepares the shared 32-coordinate view before regional L1 selection (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}), an execution order distinct from our distance-first explanation. Selection runs only during decoding, with dense prefill; gating and candidate gathering stay disabled, and archived runs do not validate current LoDA-SA.

\subsection{Distance regions and candidate budgets}
In decoding, $t$ indexes the current token across the full sequence including prefill. Let $s$ and $w$ denote the protected sink and recent-window lengths, with protected set $\mathcal P_t=\{i:0\le i\le t,\ i<s\ \text{or}\ i>t-w\}$ and unprotected middle $\mathcal M_t=\{0,\ldots,t\}\setminus\mathcal P_t$; overlapping protection counts once. The requested near length is $n_l$ tokens. The implementation uses at least one page, $L=\max(B,n_l)$, then floors the near/far cut to a page boundary: near ends at the protected window, and far is the remaining middle history.

Selected near and far pages, together with protection-boundary bypass pages, form one L2 candidate pool. There is no prescribed near/far token split: regional representation in the final set follows the scores, not a token quota. With enough positive-probability candidates, L2 cannot recover unprotected tokens from L1-rejected pages. S7 details scarce-support and masked zero-tie exceptions.

\subsection{Shared low-frequency coordinates}
Both regions and both selection stages use the same coordinate map within each layer and KV head, while each query head is scored separately. For the Qwen3-8B tail-coordinate configuration, we select post-RoPE coordinates $J=\{48,\ldots,63,112,\ldots,127\}$ from each 128-dimensional query and key. The indices retain 16 complete low-frequency split-half RoPE pairs (Supplementary Fig.~S1); this statement does not describe generic full-width, static-pair, projection or MLA configurations. With coordinate-selection matrix $E_J\in\mathbb R^{128\times32}$, the scoring vectors are $z_q=E_J^\top q$ and $z_i=E_J^\top k_i$. The retained pairs' slow positional rotation motivates this selection, although it does not guarantee preservation of important tokens. The compact implementation uses width-32 scoring tensors (S7 details the historical width-128 form).

L1 aggregates selected key coordinates into page summaries for coarse ranking; L2 refines candidates with per-token scores in the same coordinate view. Original KV remain full width. Compact scoring narrows each dot product fourfold, but view preparation and maintenance add work without compressing the original KV cache (S7).

\subsection{Regional page scoring and boundary eligibility}
Regional page caps and one shared token budget constrain the cascade (Supplementary Fig.~S2). Near pages use query--mean-key dot products to summarize page-level relevance; far pages use sign-aware extrema to bound shared-space token scores, a heuristic assignment. Summaries cover full physical pages, including protected positions (S7). In the partitioned path, a page containing a non-page-aligned sink end or recent-window start bypasses L1 ranking: it is appended outside the page quotas, and its unprotected tokens still compete in L2; only protected tokens are forced into final support. For a nonempty page $b$, let $m_{b,j}=\min_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$ and $M_{b,j}=\max_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$. Quest's construction~\cite{quest} gives
\begin{equation}
U_b(z_q)=\sum_j\bigl(z_{q,j}^{+}M_{b,j}+z_{q,j}^{-}m_{b,j}\bigr),
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $z^+=\max(z,0)$ and $z^-=\min(z,0)$. Each signed coordinate contribution is bounded by its chosen extremum, so $z_q^\top z_i\leq U_b(z_q)$ for every shared-space key in $b$. This does not bound original-dimensional logits. Extrema may come from different tokens, making the bound loose enough to misrank pages and miss important tokens; average-key scores lack this per-token bound.

\subsection{Absolute budgets and ratio-coupling contrast}
For total cap $K\geq|\mathcal P_t|$ and disjoint protection, the middle-token budget $K_{\mathrm{mid}}=K-|\mathcal P_t|$ fills one pool: L2 applies the retained-page mask, then a single TopK over group-averaged probabilities retains up to $K_{\mathrm{mid}}$ tokens regardless of region. The interface exposes absolute quantities: near page budget $n_b$, far pages $K_1-n_b$ by subtraction (page quotas clipped to availability), and near extent $n_l$. The far-page budget still depends jointly on $K_1$ and $n_b$; explicit parameters do not make regional capacities independent.

Ratio-coupled interfaces hide this subtraction. The archived parameterization derives $n_b=\max(1,\operatorname{round}(\beta K_1))$ and $n_k=\min(\lfloor\gamma n_bB\rfloor,K_{\mathrm{mid}})$ with ties-to-even rounding, so the far token budget reduces to
\begin{equation}
k_f=\max\bigl(0,\,K_{\mathrm{mid}}-\min(\lfloor\gamma n_bB\rfloor,\,K_{\mathrm{mid}})\bigr).
\label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
Three knobs, $K_1$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$, therefore reallocate far capacity through one expression, while far pages remain admitted at $k_f=0$: page eligibility and token capacity are separate constraints. Archived diagnostics exhibit all three reallocations, including an exact integer threshold at which raising $K_1$ zeroes far tokens (Q2, S6). The current interface removes the prescribed regional token split, but does not guarantee regional access: $n_b=K_1$ leaves no far-page quota, and pooled L2 can select no far tokens even when far pages are admitted.

With sufficient history and available candidate pages, an illustrative compact configuration ($K=1024$, $s=4$, $w=128$, $n_b=32$, $n_l=4096$) leaves 892 middle slots per KV head, with L1 admitting 32 near and 96 far pages (Supplementary Fig.~S2); the partial prefix page can reselect up to four protected tokens, so retained coverage is nominal.

\subsection{GQA selection and original-dimensional attention}
GQA assigns four of Qwen3’s 32 query heads to each of its eight KV heads. L1 averages their page scores before regional TopK. Ordinary L2 computes each query’s full-history logits, applies the shared page mask, then softmax. Group-averaged probabilities determine one TopK across the pooled middle tokens. L2 uses scale $1/\sqrt{128}$ before softmax even with 32 scoring coordinates. Because softmax precedes group averaging, this rule generally differs from applying softmax to group-averaged logits.

For KV group $h$, let $\mathcal S_{t,h}$ be the deduplicated union of protected positions and pooled winners, with $|\mathcal S_{t,h}|\leq K$. Each query $g$ uses this support for its own full-dimensional attention ($d=128$):
\begin{equation}
o_{h,g}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}\frac{e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{i,h}/\sqrt d}}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{j,h}/\sqrt d}}v_{i,h}.
\end{equation}
Each query applies one softmax over the merged indices, avoiding the addition of separately normalized regional outputs~\cite{salo}. Full-dimensional retained logits still use a restricted denominator, which can change the output. Physical KV gathering depends on backend and length; short sequences can use a dense masked path.

\subsection{Scope of optional far gating}
The current $n_l/n_b$ design keeps far gating disabled. A historical extension calibrates far mass from final prefill queries after per-head causal softmax, then freezes a layer decision at sparse decoding; its numeric-token-quota refill branch is not the current unified L2 rule (S5). In the unified branch, removing far-page competition does not remove all final far tokens: a sink-boundary page may still enter the pooled competition, so we claim no complete far skipping and transfer no historical gate results. Calibration thresholds do not bound decode error; quality and complete-request cost require separate evaluation.

\section{Experimental Setting}
We evaluate three aspects separately: task quality (Q1), regional candidate coverage (Q2) and execution cost (Q3). Table~\ref{tab:settings} defines the compact Qwen3-8B design: dense prefill, sparse decoding and a 1024-token cap including four prefix and 128 recent tokens (the 4/128-protection design). The grid in Q2 supplies historical descriptive comparisons, not a validated choice of current compact operating point. The current design fills $K_{\mathrm{mid}}$ by free competition; this design choice is not evidence that deleting a numeric token quota preserves quality. Archived runs retain ratio-defined geometries and numeric-token quotas; $(8,320)$ denotes near pages and near tokens, not $(n_l,n_b)$. Their quality runs applied 16-level quantize--dequantize L2 token keys outside the mainline and, for the shared-subspace arm, 128/128 protection with 32 active coordinates in zero-filled width-128 L2 keys (S7). The labels ``full-dimensional page/token scoring'' denote reported variants without complete execution configurations, not controlled ablations.

\textbf{Workloads and comparisons.} Reported quality covers five-task LongBench~\cite{longbench} and 11-task RULER~\cite{ruler} (Qwen3-8B: 32K/64K, historical 128K), plus ported Quest/AsyncTLS/JSSA baselines. FullKV is dense and uncapped (RULER/decode timing). Method comparisons must disclose native prefill/decode paths; policy-matched adaptations need separate reporting. Budget matching aligns final unique-token caps $K$ including protection; realized counts and head sharing affect KV traffic, and equal $K$ alone establishes neither quality nor cost matching (S7).

\textbf{Metrics.} Primary quality is task-score AVG over each fixed benchmark/length set; per-task gains and losses explain AVG without zero-loss requirements. Policy-matched controls require matched models, samples, lengths, scorers, phase policies and aligned sparse-token budgets; incomplete sets lack full-set AVG. Regional miss shares divide each query's missed reference mass in each region by its total mass-ranked reference mass, then average across correlated observations (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}(b)); panel (c) normalizes by full-dimensional mid-TopK mass. Neither selector time (synthetic single-layer) nor synchronized TPOT (model-level post-first-output intervals) measures preparation-inclusive complete-request latency, so unpaired studies define no quality--latency frontier; AVG differences are descriptive, not non-inferiority evidence.

\section{Evaluation and Discussion}
\subsection{Q1: Does reduced scoring width preserve task quality?}
On HotpotQA, Qasper, MuSiQue, GovReport and RepoBench-P~\cite{longbench}, five-task means fall within a 0.33-point spread: shared-subspace 47.588, the nominal page variant 47.910 and the token variant 47.666 (Supplementary Fig.~S4). These differences describe recorded configurations, not evidence that reduced width preserves quality: the variants are not verified single-factor interventions. All three arms are sparse; dense FullKV is a different reference.

\subsection{Q2: How do regional budgets constrain candidate access?}
\textbf{Descriptive grid comparisons.} An archived Qwen3-8B summary reports 86 near-page, near-extent and token-quota configurations plus one no-partition control on five LongBench tasks, with one run per configuration. Reported AVG5 is 48.264 for quota-free selection at $(n_b,n_l)=(32,4096)$, 48.300 for the best quota-free configuration and 47.462 for the control; these are descriptive point estimates. Across 69 numeric-token-quota versus quota-free comparisons covering 17 distinct $(n_b,n_l)$ settings, the mean AVG5 difference (numeric quota minus quota-free at the same geometry) is $+0.040$ points, with 42 positive differences. At $(n_b,n_l)=(32,4096)$, the numeric-quota-448 arm scores 48.668 versus 48.264 without a quota, a $+0.404$-point difference. The three paired intervals in this archived JSON summary compare the top three numeric-quota configurations with one another, not with quota-free selection; containing zero establishes neither equivalence nor the absence of a token-quota effect. These summaries do not isolate the quality contribution of near-page budget or near extent or validate the current compact pooled-L2 configuration; matched configuration and input identities, paired predictions and complete-request costs remain necessary (S6).

Archived RULER 32K arms move 58.30$\rightarrow$59.29 as near quotas shift $(16,640)\rightarrow(8,320)$ (archived $\beta=.125\!\rightarrow\!.0625$), with far pages rising 112$\rightarrow$120 and the far token budget 252$\rightarrow$572 jointly; the archived near-page-4 and near-page-8 arms respectively score 58.07 ($K_1=128$, far tokens 732) and 58.96 ($K_1=256$, near quotas $(8,320)$ with far pages 248), both below 59.29, the $K_1=256$ arm differing by CWE $+3.9$ and FWE $-9.67$. Records match; predictions are unreplayed.

\textbf{Implicit coupling failures.} Archived diagnostics exhibit all three reallocations of Eq.~(\ref{eq:coupling}). Integer enumeration of the archived formulas locates the first far-zero page cap at $K_1=156$ for $K=1024$ ($B=64$, $\beta=.125$, $\gamma=.625$, 128/128 protection); far budget 8 remains at $K_1=155$, so capacity appears to grow while far tokens vanish. Archived task scans collapse HotpotQA from 53.6--57.3 to 37.7 exactly on zero-far-budget arms, graded by far fraction (16.4 points at .875, about 1 at .125). At the archived grid cell $(\alpha,\beta)=(.25,.125)$, free competition ties the best $\gamma$ within .001 in retained-mass diagnostics (.852 versus .8515), while $\gamma=0$ reads as far-free but zeroes the near token quota, dropping retained mass to .827. The historical 128K RULER arm computed $k_f=0$ under its reported budgets (S6). These archived diagnostics unify the three anomalies but are not new controlled experiments.

Reported 32K AVG gap to dense FullKV is $-0.09$ across machines; at 64K, AVGs are 48.85/48.05 for near quotas (8,320)/(16,640) versus 48.54. Sparse arms share a machine and scorer hashes but differ in prediction scripts; the dense anchor differs in scorer and identity, and missing paired intervals preclude causal conclusions (S8).

\textbf{Ported baselines.} Author-reported AVGs (nominal 512): 56.00 (ours) versus 48.33 (AsyncTLS), a 7.67-point gap; budgets, protection and execution phases are unverified. At $K=1024$, 59.29 compares with JSSA's 58.7, Quest's 42.85 and FASA's 18.64; $K=384$ scores 15.00. S8 gives provenance, CWE's 43.4-point loss and absent paired intervals.

At 128K, the (8,320)/(16,640) arms score 45.73/46.02 on one machine; 45.73 is 98.49\% of the separate-run dense anchor 46.43. Historical RULER 128K yields 23.98 under budgets implying $k_f=0$, execution unverified, the 22.45-point loss unexplained (S6). Five-task LongBench means are 26.09 (protection-only, 256 tokens) versus 47.91 (reused sparse, 1024); retrieval and budget both change.

\subsection{Q3: Does compact scoring reduce execution cost?}
\textbf{Model-level decoding.} Sparse TPOT medians are 87.9, 97.6 and 119.9 ms at 32K, 64K and 128K versus dense 26.0, 39.7 and 67.3 ms: the reported configuration is slower at every measured length (Fig.~\ref{fig:pending-evaluation}), with no decode-time benefit. Timings include synchronization and argmax, use a non-compact configuration, and cannot locate causes or predict the compact implementation (S6).

\textbf{Local selector cost.} A separate two-round synthetic experiment (single layer, batch one, one query, 64K QKV) reports steady-state summaries of 1.3502, 1.3598 and 1.4634 ms (page, shared-subspace, token variants). Missing raw sequences and an unresolved sampling discrepancy prevent assessment (S6); they identify no bottleneck and lack quality pairing.

\subsection{Optional gate: prefill is not a decode guarantee}
Low prefill attention does not establish safe decode-time skipping. An archived input--layer record pairs a prefill far-attention proxy below .05 with a decode-tail mean of .1678 over five queries: a proxy shift, not measured quality loss. Gate thresholds .05 and .10 yield five-task AVGs of 47.876 and 47.692 against the reused 47.910 reference (three tasks decline per arm; HotpotQA 1.05 and 1.26). Neither these changes nor zero-containing bootstrap intervals establish non-inferiority; missing configurations, paired predictions and matched costs leave gated quality and net savings unverified (S6).

\textbf{Design implications and limitations.} Two constraints recur: reduced scoring width narrows each dot product while ordinary L2 still scores full history, and page-level eligibility does not guarantee final selection, which the single pool decides by group-averaged probability. A useful operating point cuts preparation-inclusive complete-request latency at matched quality. Matched measurements for the compact design remain missing; historical gate summaries lack complete configuration identity.

\section{Conclusion}
We studied LoDA-SA: shared low-frequency page/token scoring, explicit regional page budgets, one pooled token competition, and original-dimensional attention. Page eligibility does not guarantee final regional coverage. The archived grid reports quota-free AVG5 48.264 versus control 47.462, but a numeric-quota arm at the same geometry scores 48.668; these observations neither isolate quality contributions nor validate the current compact design. Historical RULER scores retain their original configurations, and the negative hybrid diagnostic, 128K loss and slower sparse decoding remain limitations. Matched quality and preparation-inclusive complete-request cost are required to establish benefit.
