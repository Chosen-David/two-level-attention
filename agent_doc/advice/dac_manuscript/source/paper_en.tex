\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous authors}}

\begin{abstract}
Sparse attention must retain useful candidates at low selection cost. We study a near--far page-to-token cascade sharing 32 low-frequency RoPE scoring coordinates and using original-dimensional attention on selected tokens. Near--far coverage involves trade-offs, and far-page reservation need not preserve far-token capacity. Author-reported 11-task RULER 32K AVG is 58.30 and 59.29 under two Qwen3-8B page/token allocations; a separate Qwen3-32B comparison reports 50.64 for sparse attention versus 52.22 for dense attention. These descriptive comparisons neither isolate mechanisms nor establish non-inferiority. Separate historical timings show slower sparse decoding. Establishing benefit for the compact 4/128-protection design still requires matched quality and preparation-inclusive complete-request cost.

\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
sparse attention, shared representation, regional retrieval, long-context inference
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
In dense long-context decoding, each new token attends to a growing history of cached keys and values. Sparse attention restricts final attention to selected tokens, but selecting them requires scoring, ranking and memory accesses. The design goal is to preserve task-relevant tokens within a fixed budget while keeping selection cheaper than the attention work it removes. Both candidate selection and final attention-set size are therefore central design problems.

Cascades such as AsyncTLS shortlist pages before selecting eligible tokens~\cite{asynctls}. Coarse ranking by cheap page summaries can omit pages carrying substantial full-attention weight. Mean pooling can dilute an isolated high token score, while sign-aware extrema bound unquantized token scores in the scoring coordinates, though that bound can be loose; neither ranks pages by their total attention weight. Following ClusterKV's token/page perspective~\cite{clusterkv}, our Qwen3 diagnostic in Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(a) exposes this mismatch: mean and extrema scoring miss 13 and 17 of 32 mass-rank reference pages, respectively. Those missed pages contain 59.65\% and 26.46\% of the reference-page attention mass, so fewer misses need not mean less missed mass. This single-query comparison motivates examining candidate access; it does not rank the selectors by task quality.

Candidate access also depends on how capacity is divided between recent and distant history. A global TopK can devote all available slots to one region, whereas regional quotas can reserve nominal capacity for each. In the diagnostic that selects whole bins in Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(b), separate near and far reservations raise the mean retained fraction of far-region attention weight from .6759 to .7207, but reduce it in 43 of 128 correlated observations. The design must address both stages: admitting far pages alone does not reserve final slots for their tokens.

We study a two-level selector that couples a shared scoring view with explicit regional budgets. Both stages use a fixed set of 32 coordinates comprising 16 complete low-frequency RoPE pairs; separate near and far page quotas control eligibility for token refinement. The coordinate diagnostic in Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(c) motivates the compact view; its random baseline does not preserve RoPE pairs. Regional token quotas then divide the remaining final budget after prefix and recent-token protection; selected tokens use original-dimensional Q/K/V in one final attention operation. Compact coordinates narrow scoring dot products, but ordinary token scoring still spans full history before masking; fewer shortlisted pages alone do not remove that work. We also formulate a prefill-informed far gate motivated by the layer variation in Figure~\ref{fig:observation}(d); it is disabled by default and requires separate evaluation.

We contribute a shared-coordinate regional selector, joint page/token capacity analysis, and separate evaluations of task quality, regional coverage and execution cost. On complete 11-task RULER32K sets, reported Qwen3-8B AVG is 0.99 points higher for near-page allocation parameter $\beta=.0625$ than for $.125$, with page and token quotas changing together. A separate 11-task RULER32K comparison on Qwen3-32B shows a 1.58-point sparse deficit relative to dense attention. These descriptive comparisons do not isolate either mechanism, and the archived sparse configuration decodes more slowly than dense attention at every measured length. Establishing a quality--latency benefit for the compact design with four-token prefix and 128-token recent-window protection requires matched task-quality and complete-request measurements, including preparation, maintenance and selection.

\section{Related Work}
Quest supplies sign-aware page-extremum bounds for far-page scoring, SparQ approximates attention from query-selected key coordinates, and HISA prunes the attention set hierarchically~\cite{quest,sparq,hisa}. AsyncTLS already combines a page-to-token cascade with coordinate selection, quantization, per-query normalization followed by GQA probability aggregation, and final attention over original KV~\cite{asynctls}. SparQ also protects a recent-token window, while HISA forces the first and last blocks into its coarse candidate set~\cite{sparq,hisa}. We study these existing components with complete low-frequency RoPE pairs shared across both stages and separate nominal near--far L1 page quotas, distinguishing page-level eligibility from final token capacity. These page quotas do not by themselves guarantee final far-token capacity; ordinary L2 still scores the full history before masking.

Reducing attention traffic also requires considering what the selector itself reads. Token-Picker uses conservative probability estimates from partial key-bit chunks to stop further key-chunk and value transfers for pruned tokens~\cite{tokenpicker}. Our selector instead ranks candidates using a fixed coordinate subset under page and token budgets; original-dimensional KV remain available for final attention. KV compression and placement methods address complementary storage and movement constraints~\cite{shadowkv,million,clusterkv}. A smaller scoring view in our design reduces neither original-KV capacity nor, by itself, complete-request latency.

\section{Near--Far Selection in a Shared Subspace}
To control both selection cost and candidate access, the cascade uses two explicit mechanisms: a shared scoring view and regional page quotas. One 32-coordinate map supports both L1 page screening and L2 token refinement, separating low-dimensional selection from original-dimensional attention (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The cascade operates only during decoding; prefill remains dense. L1 assigns separate page quotas to the near and far regions, so pages in one region do not compete for the other region's allocation. In the ordinary L2 path, full-history scoring precedes the retained-page mask and regional token selection. The selected token indices are merged and deduplicated with protected positions for attention using the original Q, K and V; recomputing retained logits does not recover omitted tokens.

\subsection{Shared low-frequency coordinates}
Both regions and both selection stages use the same coordinate map within each layer and KV head, while each query head is scored separately. For Qwen3-8B, we select post-RoPE coordinates $J=\{48,\ldots,63,112,\ldots,127\}$ from each 128-dimensional query and key. The indices retain 16 complete low-frequency split-half RoPE pairs (Supplementary Fig.~S1). With coordinate-selection matrix $E_J\in\mathbb R^{128\times32}$, the scoring vectors are $z_q=E_J^\top q$ and $z_i=E_J^\top k_i$. Their slower positional rotation motivates this selection, although it does not guarantee preservation of important tokens. The compact implementation uses width-32 scoring tensors, whereas the historical quality runs used 32 active coordinates in width-128 L2 tensors (S7).

L1 aggregates selected key coordinates into page summaries for coarse ranking; L2 refines candidates using quantize--dequantize token keys. L2 uses 16 floating-point quantize--dequantize levels, not packed four-bit storage or INT4 arithmetic (S7). The two scoring views occupy separate buffers. Original KV remain full width. Compact scoring narrows dot products, but view preparation and maintenance add work without compressing the original KV cache (S7).



\subsection{Regional page and token budgets}
In decoding, $t$ indexes the current token from zero across the full sequence including prefill, and $S=t+1$ counts valid cached positions, excluding padding. Let $s$ and $w$ denote the protected prefix and recent-window lengths. Their union and the unprotected middle are
\begin{align}
\mathcal P_t&=\{i:0\le i\le t,\ i<s\ \text{or}\ i>t-w\},\\
\mathcal M_t&=\{0,\ldots,t\}\setminus\mathcal P_t.
\end{align}
Overlapping protection counts once. Near is a suffix of $\mathcal M_t$; far is its complement there. Near excludes the protected recent window. Near uses fraction $\alpha$ of unprotected history, a one-page minimum and a downward page-aligned start; S7 specifies integer rules.

Regional page caps and token quotas constrain the cascade (Supplementary Fig.~S2). Near pages use query--mean-key dot products to summarize page-level relevance; far pages use sign-aware extrema to bound unquantized shared-space token scores. This regional assignment is a heuristic. Summaries cover full physical pages, including protected boundary positions; L2 separately limits token regions (S7). For a nonempty page $b$, let $m_{b,j}=\min_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$ and $M_{b,j}=\max_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$. Quest's construction~\cite{quest} gives
\begin{equation}
U_b(z_q)=\sum_j\bigl(z_{q,j}^{+}M_{b,j}+z_{q,j}^{-}m_{b,j}\bigr),
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $z^+=\max(z,0)$ and $z^-=\min(z,0)$. Each signed coordinate contribution is bounded by its chosen extremum, so $z_q^\top z_i\leq U_b(z_q)$ for every unquantized shared-space key in $b$. This does not bound original-dimensional logits or independently quantized scores. Extrema may come from different tokens, making the bound loose enough to misrank pages and miss important tokens. Average-key scores lack this per-token bound.

For total cap $K\geq|\mathcal P_t|$, define the middle-token budget $K_{\mathrm{mid}}=K-|\mathcal P_t|$. $\alpha$ controls near extent, $\beta$ its share of L1 page cap $K_1$, and $\gamma$ its nominal L2 quota from retained-page capacity. For the partitioned fixed-quota path (far active; no overrides), assume integer $K_1\geq1$, $\alpha>0$, $0<\beta\leq1$ and $\gamma\geq0$. With page size $B$ and sufficient regional pages/tokens, $P_n=\max(1,\operatorname{round}(\beta K_1))$, $P_f=\max(0,K_1-P_n)$, $k_n=\min(\lfloor\gamma P_nB\rfloor,K_{\mathrm{mid}})$ and $k_f=\max(0,K_{\mathrm{mid}}-k_n)$; rounding uses ties-to-even. Raising $\beta$ cannot reduce the nominal near-page quota or increase the far-page quota; rounding can leave both unchanged. Thus $P_f>0$ does not imply $k_f>0$: page and token access are separate constraints. For $K_1=128$ and $\beta=.125$, L1 reserves 16 near and 112 far pages. With enough positive-probability candidates, L2 cannot recover unprotected tokens from L1-rejected pages. S7 details scarce-support and masked zero-tie exceptions.

With disjoint protection and sufficient candidates, the compact configuration ($K=1024$, $s=4$, $w=128$) leaves 892 middle slots per KV head. Here $\gamma=.625$ yields $16\cdot64\cdot.625=640$ near and 252 far slots (Supplementary Fig.~S2). The partial prefix page can reselect up to four protected tokens, so 252 is nominal rather than guaranteed unprotected far coverage.



\subsection{GQA selection and original-dimensional attention}
Grouped-query attention (GQA) assigns four of Qwen3’s 32 query heads to each of its eight KV heads. L1 averages their page scores before regional TopK. Ordinary L2 computes each query’s full-history logits, applies the shared page mask, then softmax. Group-averaged probabilities determine regional token TopK. L2 uses scale $1/\sqrt{128}$ before softmax even with 32 scoring coordinates. Because softmax precedes group averaging, this rule generally differs from applying softmax to group-averaged logits. Width-32 scoring narrows each dot product; a smaller L1 shortlist restricts eligibility but does not reduce ordinary L2's full-history dot-product count.

For KV group $h$, let $\mathcal S_{t,h}$ be the deduplicated union of protected positions and regional winners, with $|\mathcal S_{t,h}|\leq K$. Each query $g$ uses this support for its own full-dimensional attention ($d=128$):
\begin{equation}
o_{h,g}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}\frac{e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{i,h}/\sqrt d}}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{j,h}/\sqrt d}}v_{i,h}.
\end{equation}
Each query applies one softmax over the merged indices, avoiding the addition of separately normalized regional outputs~\cite{salo}. Full-dimensional retained logits still use a restricted denominator, which can change the output. These indices define a shared logical support. Physical KV gathering and reuse depend on backend and length; short sequences can use a dense masked path.

\subsection{Prefill-informed far gating}
Beyond scoring and budgeting, layer variation offers a second savings opportunity. The optional gate tests whether low far-region attention during prefill can identify layers where far selection may be omitted during decoding; it is disabled by default. For each input and layer, a mean far-region attention proxy below $\epsilon$ sets the gate OFF; otherwise far selection stays active. Calibration uses a token-defined region that can differ from the page-aligned skipped region (S5). The first decode-path call freezes the decision until input reset (S5). OFF skips far L1/L2 scoring and selection in the supported fixed-quota path, refilling near L2 within unchanged near pages. Removing far support can change softmax denominators and group-averaged near rankings (S5). Representation preparation, sequence-wide score buffers and normalization remain.

The gate supports one sequence; the skip path requires valid length and nonempty far history (Supplementary Fig.~S3; S5). The prefill threshold does not bound decoding error; gate quality and total cost require separate evaluation.

\textbf{Workloads and comparisons.} The quality evidence comprises five LongBench tasks (question answering, summarization and code completion)~\cite{longbench}, a historical 11-task RULER~\cite{ruler} 128K comparison, and author-reported 11-task RULER 32K/64K comparisons (8B, 32B). The LongBench scores compare three nominal sparse scoring configurations; dense FullKV is a separate reference in the RULER and decode-timing records. Controlled sparse comparisons must align final unique-token cap $K$ (including protection), model, inputs and phase policy. Realized token counts and head-sharing granularity also affect KV traffic; internal page budgets need not match (S7).

\textbf{Metrics.} Primary quality is arithmetic task-score AVG over each fixed benchmark/length task set. Per-task gains and losses explain AVG; zero loss on every task is not required. Controlled comparisons require matched models, samples, lengths, scorers and phase policies, and aligned sparse-token budgets. Incomplete sets lack full-set AVG. Regional retention in Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}(b) divides selected mass by full regional mass; Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}(c) instead uses full-dimensional reference mid-TopK mass. Selector time covers a synthetic single-layer operation; synchronized TPOT measures model-level intervals after the first output token. Neither measures complete-request latency, including preparation, maintenance and input/output work. Unpaired quality and timing studies do not define a quality--latency frontier. AVG differences are descriptive, not statistical non-inferiority evidence.

\section{Evaluation and Discussion}
\subsection{Q1: Does reduced scoring width preserve task quality?}
Five-task means sit within a descriptive 0.33-point spread across the three nominal scoring configurations. On HotpotQA, Qasper, MuSiQue, GovReport and RepoBench-P~\cite{longbench}, shared-subspace scoring scores 47.588, the nominal full-dimensional page variant 47.910 and the nominal full-dimensional token variant 47.666 (Supplementary Fig.~S4). The shared-subspace score is lower by 0.322 and 0.078 points, respectively. These differences describe the recorded configurations; they do not show that reducing scoring width preserves quality. The variants are not verified single-factor interventions, and the shared-subspace run uses the earlier 128/128-protection implementation. Neither stage's representation effect nor the compact 4/128 design's quality can be inferred from this comparison. All three configurations are sparse; dense FullKV is a different reference.

\subsection{Q2: How do regional budgets constrain candidate access?}
Regional quotas lower far retention in 43/128 correlated observations despite improving its mean (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}(b)); this is not task-quality evidence. Reported 8B 32K AVG rises 58.30$\rightarrow$59.29 when $\beta=.125\!\rightarrow\!.0625$ at $K_1=128$, $K=1024$, protection 4/128. Pages 16/112$\rightarrow$8/120 and tokens 640/252$\rightarrow$320/572 change jointly. At $K_1=256$, $\beta=.03125$, near pages/tokens stay 8/320, far pages rise 120$\rightarrow$248 and far tokens stay 572, yet AVG falls to 58.96; CWE rises 3.9 and FWE falls 9.67. Data/scorer records match these sparse arms; predictions remain unreplayed.

Against dense FullKV, reported differences are $-0.09$ (8B 32K, different scorers), $-1.58$ (32B 32K, matching manifests) and $+0.31$ for the 64K $\beta=.0625$ arm over its separate-run anchor, with $\beta=.125$ at $-0.49$; the 64K sparse arms are reported on one machine (Fig.~\ref{fig:ruler32k-quality}). Cross-machine 32K comparisons, the separate-run 64K anchor and unreplayed predictions limit interpretation; S8 keeps task differences and provenance, establishing neither allocation causality nor non-inferiority.

The historical 128K sparse arm scores 23.98 against dense FullKV's 46.43. Reported budgets imply $k_f=0$ despite retained far pages, but this neither verifies execution nor explains the 22.45-point loss (S6). Protection-only scores 26.09 versus 47.91 at 256 versus 1024 tokens, jointly changing retrieval and budget.

\subsection{Q3: Does compact scoring reduce execution cost?}
\textbf{Model-level decoding.} The reported sparse configuration is \mbox{slower} than dense FullKV at every measured length (Fig.~\ref{fig:pending-evaluation}). \mbox{Sparse} TPOT medians are 87.9, 97.6 and 119.9 ms at 32K, 64K and 128K, compared with dense medians of 26.0, 39.7 and 67.3 ms. The corresponding ratios are 3.38, 2.46 and 1.78. Although the relative penalty decreases with length, absolute sparse TPOT increases. Thus these measurements show no decode-time benefit from that implementation. They include device synchronization and argmax, exclude tokenization, input transfer and text output, and use a configuration different from the compact 4/128 design. Aggregate medians cannot locate the slowdown's cause or predict the compact implementation; repetition and execution-path details are in S6.

\textbf{Local selector cost.} The shared-subspace selector has no lower reported aggregate time than the nominal full-dimensional page-scoring variant in the synthetic study. A separate two-round experiment (single layer, batch one, one query, 64K QKV) reports steady-state summaries of 1.3502 ms for nominal full-dimensional page scoring, 1.3598 ms for shared-subspace scoring and 1.4634 ms for nominal full-dimensional token scoring. Relative to nominal page scoring, shared-subspace scoring is 0.0237 ms slower in the first round and 0.0046 ms faster in the second, showing no consistent timing advantage across rounds. Missing raw sample sequences and an unresolved sampling-protocol discrepancy prevent a reliable assessment of these small differences or a conclusion of timing equivalence (S6). These local timings do not identify a bottleneck, are not paired with LongBench quality evaluation and do not measure complete-request cost.

\subsection{Optional gate: prefill is not a decode guarantee}
Low prefill attention does not establish that far selection can be safely skipped during decoding. An archived input--layer record pairs a prefill far-attention proxy below .05 with a reported decode-tail mean of .1678 over the last five queries; this is a proxy shift, not measured task-quality loss. Separately, reported gate thresholds .05 and .10 yield arithmetic five-task AVGs of 47.876 and 47.692 against a reused 47.910 reference: decreases of .034 and .218 points. AVG is the primary quality measure. Three tasks decline in each arm, including HotpotQA by 1.05 and 1.26 points; these differences describe heterogeneity, not a per-task zero-loss requirement. Neither small AVG changes nor task-bootstrap intervals containing zero establish non-inferiority. Missing complete scoring configurations, paired predictions and matched costs leave gated shared-subspace quality and net savings unverified (S6).

\textbf{Design implications and limitations.} Scoring and allocation rules expose two distinct constraints. Reducing scoring width narrows each dot product, but ordinary L2 still scores and normalizes the full history. Far-page quotas admit candidates to refinement without guaranteeing final far-token capacity. A useful operating point reduces complete-request latency at matched task quality, counting preparation, maintenance and selection once. The reported historical sparse configuration decodes more slowly than dense attention; separately measured selector times establish no complete-request latency benefit. Matched task-quality and complete-request measurements for the compact 4/128 design remain missing; historical gate summaries also lack complete shared-subspace configuration identity.

\section{Conclusion}
We studied shared low-frequency page/token scoring and regional allocation, retaining original-dimensional attention on selected tokens. The analysis separates narrower scoring from reduced execution work, and far-page reservation from far-token coverage. Regional quotas lower far retention in some Qwen3 diagnostic observations. Author-reported Qwen3-8B RULER 32K AVG (11 tasks) is 0.99 points higher at $\beta=.0625$ than at $\beta=.125$, with page and token quotas both changed. The larger nominal far-page-budget arm reports 58.96, 0.33 below $\beta=.0625$'s 59.29 at identical nominal token quotas. A separate Qwen3-32B sparse configuration trails dense attention by 1.58 points. These descriptive results neither isolate mechanisms nor establish non-inferiority. Historical 128K quality loss and slower sparse decoding remain limitations of their reported configurations. Establishing benefit for the compact 4/128 design still requires matched quality and complete-request cost, including preparation, maintenance and selection.
