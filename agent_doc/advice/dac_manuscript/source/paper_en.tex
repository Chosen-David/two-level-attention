\begin{abstract}
Page screening in sparse attention can discard useful distant tokens before refinement, making its scoring representation a critical part of candidate selection. We study a near--far cascade whose page screening and token refinement share 32 low-frequency RoPE coordinates, arranged as 16 complete rotation pairs. Separate regional page quotas retain candidate access to near and far history, while final attention uses the selected original-dimensional keys and values. In an archived configuration with 128-token prefix and recent protection, on five LongBench tasks with Qwen3-8B, the shared-subspace configuration uses 32 active coordinates for both page and token scoring and averages 47.588. Nominal variants with full-dimensional page scoring (128/32 active coordinates) or token scoring (32/128) score 47.910 and 47.666, respectively. The available records do not establish matched configurations, so these score differences are descriptive. A separate synthetic study reports no selector-latency reduction for the shared-subspace configuration relative to the nominal page-scoring variant. The results motivate evaluating compact scoring jointly with selection quality and preparation-inclusive cost, without establishing a joint quality--latency advantage.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
sparse attention, shared representation, regional retrieval, long-context inference
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Long-context sparse attention must retain useful history while making selection cheaper than the attention it removes. In a two-level selector, page screening determines which tokens reach refinement; the next stage cannot recover a rejected page. Scoring dimensionality controls arithmetic per comparison, while preparation, ranking and memory access also contribute to latency. We study scoring representation and candidate allocation as distinct design choices.

We study three connected choices: shared scoring coordinates, regional candidate quotas, and an optional prefill-informed far gate (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The default near--far cascade uses the first two; the gate is a separate extension. Each layer and KV head uses one set of complete RoPE coordinate pairs for page screening and token refinement. The cascade assigns separate page quotas to recent unprotected history and the remaining history, allowing distant candidates to enter refinement even when the near region dominates page selection. Original-dimensional Q/K/V determine attention over the resulting token set. Sharing the scoring view reduces representational duplication in the design; regional quotas control candidate access. Neither mechanism alone guarantees lower latency or unchanged task quality.

This design raises three questions: whether compact coordinates retain useful candidates, whether regional quotas improve retrieval at a fixed final token budget, and whether the attention saved exceeds representation preparation and selection costs. Figure~\ref{fig:observation} presents four motivating observations on Qwen3-8B (four source sequences; the 128 query positions are repeated observations, not independent samples). Attention mass is position-segmented (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}a): on a representative 14K query, the far region carries about half of the unprotected mid mass over 76\% of mid tokens, concentrated in sparse spikes, while the near region is dense and uniform. The same panel stacks three single-pool selections, all with a 2048 mid-token budget: an oracle upper bound ranked by true mass, page-mean (avg) scoring in the shared 32-dimensional subspace, and a Quest-style per-dimension minmax bound. Averaging dilutes spike pages: of the 33 oracle-selected pages, avg misses 13, which account for 59\% of the oracle mass; in the spike page at position 7936 a single token carries 100\% of the page mass, and its page mean is diluted by the other 63 tokens, while the minmax bound---never below the largest in-page token score---rescues 3 of the 4 missed far pages. This motivates heterogeneous scorers: block averages near, a no-miss bound far. Even an oracle quality-ranked single pool under-serves the far region (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}b): mean far capture is 0.69 versus 0.93 near, 0.41 for queries with diffuse far mass, and 0 for the worst query; a reserved far quota lifts the worst case to 0.26, so regional quotas act as anti-eviction floors rather than average-case gains. At an equal 32/128 scoring budget, complete low-frequency RoPE pairs retain 0.84 of the reference top-$K$ mass, unpaired random coordinates 0.51 and high-frequency coordinates 0.24 (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}c). Finally, prefill far mass is highly uneven across layers---24 of 36 layers have per-sequence medians below $0.01$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:observation}d)---motivating the per-layer far gate studied as an approximation policy in Section~III. These oracle and diagnostic-scorer simulations are upper bounds or offline illustrations over four source sequences; they do not measure production scorer fidelity, task quality, or decode-time drift, and Supplementary Note~S6 reports a counterexample in which prefill statistics fail to bound decode-time far demand.

\section{Related Work}
Query-aware retrieval methods such as Quest, SparQ and HISA reduce the active attention set through approximate selection~\cite{quest,sparq,hisa}. AsyncTLS provides a page-to-token cascade for sparse selection~\cite{asynctls}. We ask whether a shared low-frequency RoPE scoring view and separate near--far page quotas can retain useful candidates at a fixed final token budget. This separates scoring representation and regional allocation from execution scheduling; their quality and preparation-inclusive cost require matched evaluation.

KV compression and placement methods address complementary memory-capacity and data-movement constraints~\cite{shadowkv,million,clusterkv}. The method studied here preserves original keys and values and changes their selection. Consequently, it does not inherit a reduction in original-KV cache capacity from the compact scoring representation.

\section{Near--Far Selection in a Shared Subspace}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates shared-subspace scoring from original-dimensional attention. The page-screening stage (L1) reserves near and far pages; the token-refinement stage (L2) ranks tokens using the same coordinates. Selected token indices and protection define the original-K/V attention set, with a separate original Q for each query. Regional quotas govern candidate access, while ordinary L2 computes full-history scores before masking.

\subsection{Shared low-frequency coordinates}
For Qwen3-8B, the evaluated representation selects post-RoPE coordinates
$J=\{48,\ldots,63,112,\ldots,127\}$ from each 128-dimensional query and key. These are 16 complete low-frequency rotation pairs, rather than a contiguous suffix or a learned projection. Their slower positional phase variation motivates examining them as a compact \mbox{scoring} view; it does not imply greater retrieval fidelity, which depends on the query and key content. The current shared-subspace implementation stores 32 coordinates compactly; the archived LongBench implementation instead used 32 active coordinates in 128-wide L2 tensors (Supplementary Note~S7). With coordinate-selection matrix $E_J\in\mathbb R^{128\times32}$, the scoring views are $z_q=E_J^\top q$ and $z_i=E_J^\top k_i$. A layer and KV head prepare one key view for both regions and both stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:detail-subspace}; Supplementary Fig.~S1). Query heads retain their own scores. Original keys and values remain available at full width for final attention and later queries; this design does not reduce original-KV cache capacity.





Both stages use the same fixed coordinate map $E_J$. For key matrix $\mathbf K$ and regional row selector $R$, $R(\mathbf K E_J)=(R\mathbf K)E_J$: regional selection preserves the scoring coordinates. L1 derives page summaries and L2 derives quantize--dequantize token keys from those coordinates, with no requirement to share a physical buffer. The final token mask addresses original full-width keys and values. Appended keys, moving regional boundaries and coordinate-map changes require consistent derived views, logical token indices and region assignments; the identity itself does not prescribe incremental maintenance. Net latency depends on preparing and maintaining these views, selection and backend KV access, and has not been shown to decrease.

\subsection{Regional page and token budgets}
During decoding, let $t$ be the current token's zero-based position in the full sequence, including prefill tokens, so that $S=t+1$ counts valid cached positions, including the current position and excluding padding. Let $s$ and $w$ denote the protected prefix and recent-window lengths. Their union and the unprotected middle are
\begin{align}
\mathcal P_t&=\{i:0\le i\le t,\ i<s\ \text{or}\ i>t-w\},\\
\mathcal M_t&=\{0,\ldots,t\}\setminus\mathcal P_t.
\end{align}
Overlapping protection counts once. Near is a suffix of the unprotected middle history; far is its complement. Near is therefore distinct from the protected sliding window. The near boundary follows fraction $\alpha$ of unprotected history under minimum-page and alignment rules. The unprotected middle has length $\max(0,S-|\mathcal P_t|)$; the selected configuration is specified in the experimental setting.

Figure~\ref{fig:detail-near_far} details the regional cascade. Near uses average-key page scores. Far uses sign-aware extrema in the shared space. For each nonempty region-intersected page $b$, define $m_{b,j}=\min_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$ and $M_{b,j}=\max_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$. Quest's extrema construction gives
\begin{equation}
U_b(z_q)=\sum_j\bigl(z_{q,j}^{+}M_{b,j}+z_{q,j}^{-}m_{b,j}\bigr),
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $z^+=\max(z,0)$ and $z^-=\min(z,0)$. This bounds unquantized shared-space dot products, not original-dimensional logits or separately quantized scores. Extrema can come from different tokens; a top-page shortlist can still miss important tokens. Average-key scores do not inherit this bound.



With $K_1=128$ and $\beta=.125$, near and far receive 16 and 112 L1 pages. Regional reservation allocates access, not a quality guarantee: a far page can displace a useful near page. A later token quota cannot recover a token after L1 discarded its page. This distinguishes regional L1 screening from splitting one global shortlist after selection.

For each KV head $h$, the intended regional support consists of valid tokens in retained pages. The example below assumes this support can fill each quota. For the supported budget domain $K\geq|\mathcal P_t|$, the middle cap is $K_{\mathrm{mid}}=K-|\mathcal P_t|$. In the sufficient-length configuration derived from the implementation, a per-head total cap $K=1024$ leaves 892 nominal middle-token slots (Supplementary Fig.~S2). In the standard configuration with sufficient eligible candidates, $\gamma=.625$ acts on near-page capacity, yielding $16\cdot64\cdot.625=640$ near tokens and 252 far slots. The selected configuration protects four prefix and 128 recent tokens. Because four prefix tokens occupy only part of a page, far selection can reselect up to four protected positions; 252 is a nominal quota, not a guaranteed count of unprotected far tokens. Scarce-support and zero-tie behavior are detailed in Supplementary Note~S7.

\subsection{GQA selection and original-dimensional attention}
With grouped-query attention (GQA), 32 query heads and 8 KV heads give four query heads per KV group. L1 averages their page scores before regional TopK. Ordinary L2 scores the full history for each query, applies the shared L1 mask, and averages the independently normalized query probabilities within each KV group. In this Qwen3 implementation, L2 logits retain the original-head scale $1/\sqrt{128}$ before per-query softmax, including when scoring uses 32 coordinates. This probability aggregation differs from applying softmax to averaged logits. Sharing coordinates therefore preserves query-specific distributions, while full-history scoring means that a smaller page shortlist does not itself reduce L2 dot-product work.

\begin{samepage}
The group's unique selected set $\mathcal S_{t,h}$ combines protection and regional winners under the total cap. All queries $g$ in that group use these indices, but recompute their own full-dimensional attention, with head width $d=128$:
\begin{equation}
o_{h,g}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}\frac{e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{i,h}/\sqrt d}}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{j,h}/\sqrt d}}v_{i,h}.
\end{equation}
\end{samepage}
Merging token indices before this softmax avoids adding separately normalized regional outputs~\cite{salo}. Retained logits use original KV, but restricting the denominator can change attention output. Selected token indices define a common original-space attention set. Physical gathering and reuse depend on the attention backend and sequence length; the inspected implementation also has a short-sequence dense fallback. 

\subsection{Prefill-informed far gating}
The optional prefill-informed gate (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) asks whether prefill observations can identify layers where repeated far selection can be omitted. Per input and layer, mean prefill far-attention weight sum below $\epsilon$ sets OFF; otherwise far selection stays active. The decision freezes at decode entry and is reused. The feature is disabled by default. In the supported fixed-quota implementation, OFF skips both far scoring stages and their selections, while unused L2 quota refills near candidates without expanding the near-page budget. Representation preparation and sequence-wide score buffers and normalization remain.

Figure~\ref{fig:detail-gate} shows the gate decision and refill. We implement this extension for a single sequence; Supplementary Note~S5 gives the observation boundary and state transitions. Its threshold controls a prefill statistic rather than subsequent attention error. The gate is therefore an approximation policy whose effect on quality and total cost must be evaluated separately from the default ungated method.

\section{Experimental Setting}
The selected Qwen3-8B candidate uses dense prefill and sparse decoding, protecting the first four attention-sink tokens and the most recent 128 tokens (Table~\ref{tab:settings}). Their deduplicated union counts toward the total token cap $K=1024$; content-based selection allocates the remaining slots. The archived five-task scores below retain their original configuration evidence: the shared-subspace run used 128/128 protection and 128-wide, zero-filled L2 keys with 32 active coordinates. Those scores do not validate the selected 4/128 candidate. The selector uses 16-level quantize--dequantize values in floating-point tensors, distinct from packed INT4 arithmetic. \textbf{Representation and quality.} Dense FullKV retains the full attention support and is the quality reference. The full-dimensional sparse control instead retains sparse selection with full-dimensional scores in both stages. A controlled L1/L2 factorial comparison requires all four modes with fixed scoring rules, protected sets and model settings; the descriptive scores below do not complete that comparison. For sparse baselines, fairness aligns the final unique-token cap $K$ per query-attention row, including protection, on the same model, inputs, decoding phase and dense-layer policy. In our selector, each KV group uses one common selected set; equal $K$ does not imply equal support unions across query heads or equal KV traffic. Internal candidate-page quotas remain method parameters and need not match across methods; their scoring and memory costs are included. A matched comparison must report realized token counts, page rounding and per-query-head versus shared-KV-head selection separately (Supplementary Note~S7).

\textbf{Comparison design.} Replay with fixed token indices isolates reuse cost by comparing reuse and reconstruction over the same decode horizon, including initial preparation and per-step maintenance, under the same coordinate map, summary and quantization rules, selected token indices and final attention. To isolate L1 regional allocation, the regional/global comparison instead fixes page size, total L1 cap $K_1$, representation, protection, L2 refinement and its token quotas, and final cap $K$; only L1 regional allocation changes. Both configurations use the same page scorer across the history, and any candidate shortage is reflected in realized token counts. These ablation controls do not require competing sparse methods to share $K_1$. Gate comparisons retain active far selection, far exclusion with near-quota reallocation, and far exclusion without reallocation because removing far can also change the near ranking.

\textbf{Cost and uncertainty.} Complete request time includes representation preparation, maintenance, calibration, selection, KV gathers, attention and synchronization, with overlapping work counted once. TTFT measures time to the first returned token; TPOT averages subsequent return intervals. A task-quality/latency comparison requires the same model, inputs, generation settings and scoring procedure. Uncertainty in task-score differences must account for repeated questions from the same source document. Non-inferiority additionally requires an acceptable-loss margin specified before examining confirmation results. The present analysis reports representation scores and local selector timings separately; it does not estimate a joint end-to-end quality--latency frontier. Supplementary Note~S6 records the experiment identities and reproducibility limits.

\section{Preliminary Results}
\textbf{Reported configuration scores.} Across HotpotQA, Qasper, MuSiQue, GovReport and RepoBench-P~\cite{longbench}, the mean scores are 47.588 (shared-subspace configuration), 47.910 (nominal full-dimensional page-scoring variant) and 47.666 (nominal full-dimensional token-scoring variant). Relative to the shared-subspace configuration, the nominal page- and token-scoring variants are higher by 0.322 and 0.078 points; the latter is 0.244 points below the former. Neither variant is verified as a controlled change in scoring dimensionality. Their score differences are therefore descriptive and do not isolate either stage's effect (Supplementary Fig.~S4 and Note~S7). The archived shared-subspace configuration score does not directly validate the current compact implementation. None is dense FullKV.

The current selected shared-subspace configuration remains unmeasured against dense FullKV under verified matching conditions. Figure~\ref{fig:fullkv-quality} preserves the historical 128K scores. Applying the inspected quota formula to the reported $\beta=.375$, $K_1=128$, page size 64, $\gamma=.625$ and 128/128 protection gives a nominal far-token quota of zero within the 768-token middle budget. This code/configuration inference does not verify the executed path or isolate the cause of the score gap. Separately, the five-task configuration means cannot establish preservation of dense-attention quality. Task-level bootstrap summaries describe variation across these five tasks, rather than a document-paired non-inferiority test.

\textbf{Model-level decode diagnostic.} Using dense attention at each input length as the reference, Figure~\ref{fig:pending-evaluation} compares author-reported dynamic-cache TPOT for the archived sparse configuration. At 32K, 64K and 128K, sparse medians are 87.9, 97.6 and 119.9 ms, versus 26.0, 39.7 and 67.3 ms for dense attention: sparse-to-dense ratios of reported medians of 3.38, 2.46 and 1.78. Across these three reported lengths, the ratio decreases while sparse TPOT rises and remains above dense TPOT. This synchronized diagnostic excludes tokenization, input transfer and text output; it is not complete-request latency. These aggregate medians do not identify a causal bottleneck; raw repetitions have not been recovered in the inspected snapshot and runtime routes remain unverified. The timings are not linked to matched quality runs or verified as measurements of the selected 4/128 candidate.

\textbf{Local selector cost.} A separate synthetic experiment uses single-layer, batch-one, single-query QKV at length 64K. Its steady-state timing summaries from two rounds are 1.3502 ms for nominal full-dimensional page scoring, 1.3598 ms for shared-subspace scoring and 1.4634 ms for nominal full-dimensional token scoring. The shared-subspace selector thus has no lower reported time than the nominal page-scoring variant in this setting. This ordering does not identify a limiting stage, and the unresolved sampling-protocol discrepancy prevents a reliable interpretation of small timing differences (Supplementary Note~S6). The operator timings are not paired with the LongBench quality runs and do not measure complete-request cost.

\textbf{Limits of protection and prefill gating.} The protection-only configuration scores 26.09 against a 47.91 reference, while using 256 rather than 1024 tokens. The difference therefore combines retrieval-policy and budget effects. Prefill statistics also fail to bound later far-region demand: an observed input--layer pair with prefill far-attention weight sum below .05 reaches .1678 during decoding. Relative to the reported reference score of 47.910, gate thresholds of .05 and .10 are associated with mean decreases of .034 and .218 points, respectively; three of five tasks decline in each configuration, including HotpotQA decreases of 1.05 and 1.26 points. The larger per-task losses demonstrate that a small average change can conceal task sensitivity. The complete scoring configuration is not identified in these gate summaries, so the results cannot be attributed to the gated shared-subspace method (Supplementary Note~S6).

\textbf{Scope.} The results describe configuration scores and the limitations of prefill-based gating on the studied Qwen3 configurations. They do not establish non-inferiority to dense FullKV, the isolated benefit of regional page quotas, or lower complete-request cost. The available 128K RULER summaries~\cite{ruler} do not establish a verified matched comparison for the current compact configuration (Supplementary Note~S6). Conclusions are consequently limited to the reported configurations and workloads.

\section{Conclusion}
We studied near--far selection with one low-frequency RoPE scoring view shared across both stages. The design reduces scoring width while retaining original-dimensional KV and, in the ordinary implementation, full-history token scoring. Reported configuration-score differences do not isolate stage effects, and the separate synthetic timing study reports no latency advantage for shared-subspace scoring. Whether these representation and allocation choices offer a competitive trade-off therefore depends on task quality and preparation-inclusive request cost under matched final unique token budgets, with acceptable quality degradation specified before evaluation.
