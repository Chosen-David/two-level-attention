\begin{abstract}
Long-context sparse attention must retain useful history without spending more on selection than it saves in attention. We present LoDA-SA, a two-level selector that reuses complete low-frequency RoPE coordinate pairs for regional page ranking and pooled token selection, followed by full-dimensional attention on the selected support. Explicit page budgets expose candidate access, while pooled selection removes prescribed regional token quotas. On Qwen3-8B, reported single-run pooled configurations attain 11-task RULER means of 59.02, 47.80 and 46.82 at 32K, 64K and 128K, compared with separate-run dense means of 59.38, 48.54 and 46.43. A region-specific advantage of the scoring rules has not been established, and unmatched execution identities prevent non-inferiority claims. On the primary H20-3e environment, sparse CUDA-Graph implementations report lower steady-state TPOT than archived dense-eager baselines, but excluded preparation and unmatched quality/timing settings preclude a complete-request quality--cost claim.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
sparse attention, shared representation, regional retrieval, long-context inference
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Long-context decoding requires access to useful history while limiting attention cost. Under a finite token budget, a selector must decide which parts of the history can supply candidates and how to rank them. Global ranking does not reserve access for any positional region. Distance-aware allocation makes this choice explicit, without assuming that importance decreases monotonically with distance. Its value depends on both the information retained and the cost of finding it.

Hierarchical selectors shortlist pages before refining individual tokens. AsyncTLS combines inexpensive page summaries with token-level refinement~\cite{asynctls}. Mean pooling and sign-aware extrema do not rank pages by their total attention weight. We therefore distinguish page admission from token competition, without assuming that a regional scoring rule establishes better retrieval.

We study LoDA-SA, a two-level selector that combines a shared scoring view with explicit regional page budgets. Its compact Qwen3 configuration uses 32 coordinates comprising 16 complete low-frequency RoPE pairs at both stages. After preparing this view, LoDA-SA ranks near and far pages with averaging and sign-aware extrema, respectively. Absolute near extent $n_l$ and near page budget $n_b$ expose the allocation, with far-page capacity set by subtraction. Nonaligned protection-boundary pages enter outside the page quotas. L2 selects from one pooled token competition, followed by original-dimensional attention over the merged support.

We evaluate the currently selected pooled configuration, $(n_b,n_l)=(32,4096)$ with regional token quotas disabled. This is the selected operating point, not a claim of a globally optimal configuration. Its reported RULER results and Graph implementation timings are considered separately because their complete execution identities and measurement protocols are not matched.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Shared low-frequency selection.} We use one compact coordinate view for page ranking and token refinement while retaining original-dimensional Q/K/V for final attention.
\item \textbf{Explicit regional budgets.} We expose near extent and page allocation, separate page admission from pooled L2 selection, and specify the resulting support and capacity constraints.
\item \textbf{Graph-based implementation and evaluation.} We implement pooled selection with a static cache and whole-step CUDA Graph, and evaluate steady-state decoding across three model configurations alongside the selected strategy's task-level quality. These measurements do not establish complete-request or matched quality--cost gains.
\end{itemize}
Task-score proximity alone is insufficient for acceleration. Ordinary L2 still scores full history before masking, so fewer shortlisted pages do not eliminate that work. Whole-step CUDA Graph changes execution overhead, but a quality--latency benefit requires matched quality and complete-request timing, including representation preparation.


\section{Related Work}
Quest supplies sign-aware page-extremum bounds, SparQ selects key coordinates from queries, and HISA prunes the attention set hierarchically~\cite{quest,sparq,hisa}. AsyncTLS explicitly follows Quest for coarse block selection and applies Double Sparsity for fine token selection; its cascade also includes quantization, per-query normalization with GQA probability aggregation and original-KV attention~\cite{asynctls}. Earlier Twilight uses a select-then-prune architecture~\cite{twilight}; LoDA-SA studies explicit distance-region page access within this hierarchical-selection paradigm, with complete low-frequency RoPE pairs shared across both stages and unified L2 competition. The relationship is algorithmic; code lineage is unverified. JSSA learns a shared query--key projection and uses gather-free indexed attention~\cite{jssa}; LoDA-SA instead fixes complete low-frequency RoPE pairs and controls page access by distance region.

The selector's own reads also matter. Token-Picker uses conservative probability estimates from partial key-bit chunks to stop further key-chunk and value transfers for pruned tokens~\cite{tokenpicker}; our selector instead ranks candidates with a fixed coordinate subset under page budgets, keeping original-dimensional KV. Our selector keeps both selection stages in bf16: AsyncTLS token keys are INT4, FFD scans 2-bit keys, and official HISA pools block summaries in FP8~\cite{asynctls,ffd,hisa}; LoDA-SA targets lower selection overhead through compact scoring and structured candidate admission, not numeric compression, and ordinary L2 still scores full history, so narrowing alone establishes no saving. KV compression and placement methods address complementary storage and movement constraints~\cite{shadowkv,million,clusterkv}; a smaller scoring view retains original-KV capacity without establishing lower complete-request latency.

\section{LoDA-SA: Distance-Aware Selection in a Shared Subspace}
LoDA-SA uses a two-level decode-time selector followed by full-dimensional attention over the selected tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The reference configuration prepares a shared 32-coordinate scoring view, ranks near and far pages separately (L1), and selects tokens in a single pool after page masking (L2). The near extent $n_l$ sets the regional boundary; $n_b$ assigns near pages from $K_1$ ranked-page slots, not token slots. Prefill remains dense. Far gating and L2 candidate gathering are disabled in this reference path.

\subsection{Distance regions and candidate budgets}
During decoding, $t$ is the zero-based index of the current token, including prefill positions. Let $s$ and $w$ denote the protected sink and recent-window lengths, with protected set $\mathcal P_t=\{i:0\le i\le t,\ i<s\ \text{or}\ i>t-w\}$ and unprotected middle $\mathcal M_t=\{0,\ldots,t\}\setminus\mathcal P_t$; overlapping protection counts once. History is divided into $B$-token pages; the final page may be incomplete. For requested near extent $n_l$, the implementation sets $L=\max(B,n_l)$ and rounds the near/far cut down to a page boundary, so the realized extent can differ from $n_l$. The near region lies immediately before the protected recent window, and the far region covers earlier middle history. Pages straddling a protection boundary require separate page- and token-eligibility rules; S3 gives the exact integer intervals.

The union of regionally selected pages and protection-boundary bypass pages defines the L2 page mask.  L2 still computes logits over the full history; the page mask limits positive-probability positions rather than the scope of logit computation. If positive-probability positions fill the pooled TopK count, masked positions cannot win; otherwise, zero-valued ties may admit positions from rejected pages (S3).

\subsection{Shared low-frequency coordinates}
Within each layer and KV head, near and far scoring share one coordinate map across L1 and L2; query heads are scored separately. The Qwen3-8B tail-coordinate configuration selects post-RoPE coordinates $J=\{48,\ldots,63,112,\ldots,127\}$ from each 128-dimensional query and key. These coordinates form 16 complete low-frequency RoPE pairs under the split-half convention (Supplementary Note S1). With coordinate-selection matrix $E_J\in\mathbb R^{128\times32}$, the scoring vectors are $z_q=E_J^\top q$ and $z_i=E_J^\top k_i$. Their slow positional rotation motivates the choice, but does not guarantee retention of tokens favored by full-dimensional attention. These indices and pairing semantics describe the Qwen3-8B split-half RoPE path, not generic full-width, static-pair, learned-projection or MLA configurations. The reference path uses width-32 scoring tensors; S3 specifies the integer boundaries and support exceptions.

 The view reduces each scoring contraction from 128 to 32 coordinates, but this alone establishes no speedup: preparing and maintaining scoring tensors adds work, while original KV remain full-dimensional.

\subsection{Regional page scoring and boundary eligibility}
Regional ranked-page quotas and one shared token budget constrain the cascade (Supplementary Fig.~S2). For a nonempty page $b$, the near score is $A_b(z_q)=z_q^\top\bar z_b$, where $\bar z_b=|b|^{-1}\sum_{i\in b}z_i$. Far pages instead use sign-aware extrema. We use this near-mean/far-extrema assignment as a heuristic; a region-specific advantage of either statistic is not established. Summaries cover full physical pages, including protected positions (S3). Pages straddling nonaligned protection boundaries are excluded from regional L1 ranking and added to its output mask outside the quotas; their unprotected tokens still compete in L2. For a nonempty page $b$, let $m_{b,j}=\min_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$ and $M_{b,j}=\max_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$. Quest's construction~\cite{quest} gives
\begin{equation}
U_b(z_q)=\sum_j\bigl(z_{q,j}^{+}M_{b,j}+z_{q,j}^{-}m_{b,j}\bigr),
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $z^+=\max(z,0)$ and $z^-=\min(z,0)$ act coordinatewise. The bound applies to every key in the selected coordinate space: $z_q^\top z_i\leq U_b(z_q)$ for $i\in b$. This bound does not bound original-dimensional logits or page attention mass. Extrema from different tokens can make $U_b$ loose and alter page rankings relative to their maximum token scores; mean-key scores have no analogous per-token upper bound.

\subsection{Page quotas and pooled token budget}
For total cap $K\geq|\mathcal P_t|$, the nominal pooled budget is $K_{\mathrm{mid}}=K-|\mathcal P_t|$. After page masking and per-query softmax, probabilities averaged within each KV group determine up to $K_{\mathrm{mid}}$ pooled winners, regardless of region. Pooled TopK can reselect protected positions when the prefix ends inside a page, leaving fewer than $K_{\mathrm{mid}}$ distinct unprotected tokens after deduplication (S3). Explicit page quotas expose a dependence rather than remove it: increasing the near quota reduces far-page capacity at fixed $K_1$. The ranked-page quotas are $n_b$ near and $K_1-n_b$ far, each clipped to availability; $n_l$ sets the requested near extent.

Pooled L2 does not require both regions to contribute: $n_b=K_1$ leaves no ranked far-page quota, and admitted far pages may yield no final winners. Boundary bypass adds eligible pages independently of ranked quotas; it does not reserve unprotected token slots.

For example, with nonoverlapping protection and sufficient eligible pages, $K=1024$, $s=4$, $w=128$, $K_1=128$, $n_b=32$ and $n_l=4096$ give 892 nominal pooled slots per KV head and ranked-page quotas of 32 near and 96 far (Supplementary Fig.~S2). Boundary bypass pages enter in addition to these quotas.

\subsection{GQA selection and full-dimensional attention}
Qwen3-8B assigns four of its 32 query heads to each of its eight KV heads. L1 averages their page scores before regional TopK. L2 computes full-history logits for each query head, applies the shared page mask, then performs per-head softmax. Group-averaged probabilities determine the pooled TopK. L2 uses scale $1/\sqrt{128}$ before softmax even with 32 scoring coordinates. Because softmax precedes group averaging, this rule generally differs from applying softmax to group-averaged logits.

For KV group $h$, let $\mathcal S_{t,h}$ be the deduplicated union of protected positions and pooled winners, with $|\mathcal S_{t,h}|\leq K$. Each query head $g$ in group $h$ computes full-dimensional attention on this support ($d=128$):
\begin{equation}
o_{h,g}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}\frac{e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{i,h}/\sqrt d}}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{j,h}/\sqrt d}}v_{i,h}.
\end{equation} Simply adding separately normalized regional outputs would not implement this attention; split computation requires denominator-aware recombination, as in SALO~\cite{salo}. Restricting the support can still change the output relative to dense attention. Physical gathering of selected K/V entries is backend-dependent; short sequences may use a dense masked path.

\subsection{Support constraints}
Near-mean/far-extrema scoring is a heuristic whose benefit has not been isolated by matched task-level ablation.

Explicit page budgets make regional access inspectable but do not guarantee regional representation in the final support. At fixed $K_1$, increasing $n_b$ reduces the ranked far-page quota; within the admitted support, one pooled competition may select no far tokens. Nonaligned protection-boundary pages enter outside those quotas. The implementation also clips TopK by interval width rather than positive support, allowing masked zero ties to win when positive support is scarce (S3). The occurrence and quality impact of that behavior in T6 have not been measured. Thus the budget definition and observed task scores do not establish a universal coverage guarantee.

\section{Experimental Setting}
The evaluated strategy is the currently selected pooled configuration: page size 64, $K_1=128$, $K=1024$, near pages/extent $(32,4096)$, 4/128 sink/recent protection, and no prescribed regional token quota. \section{Evaluation and Discussion}
\subsection{Q1: What quality does sparse retrieval retain?}
\textbf{Current pooled-selection records.} Reported configurations at $(n_b,n_l)=(32,4096)$ attain RULER means of 59.02, 47.80 and 46.82 at 32K, 64K and 128K. Relative to separately recorded dense means of 59.38, 48.54 and 46.43, differences computed before rounding are $-0.36$, $-0.74$ and $+0.38$ points. Each length has one reported run and 100 examples for each of the 11 tasks. These observations directly concern pooled token selection; they establish neither non-inferiority nor the isolated effect of replacing fixed quotas. S4 preserves the reported configuration, all task scores and execution-identity limits.

At 128K, MultiKey-2 falls from 57.00 with dense attention to 39.00 with pooled selection ($-18.00$ points), while FWE rises from 13.00 to 37.33 ($+24.33$). These task changes partly offset in the $+0.38$ macro difference; the aggregate does not imply uniform task preservation. Figure~\ref{fig:current-quality} shows all 33 differences, and S4 maps every point to its task and both arm scores.

\subsection{Q2: How does the Graph implementation perform?}
\textbf{Graph-based decoding.} On the primary H20-3e environment, the batch-one Graph implementation achieves mean steady-state TPOTs of 24.70, 27.40 and 32.42 ms at nominal 32K, 64K and 128K, against archived dense means of 26.28, 39.67 and 67.04 ms. The sparse arm uses a static cache and whole-step CUDA Graph; the dense baseline uses eager execution and DynamicCache. Graph TPOT excludes cache filling and capture. The runs therefore establish neither a graph-versus-graph comparison nor preparation-inclusive request savings. A separately reported local-GPU1 replica has higher sparse TPOT at 32K (47.61 versus 39.34 ms); the source attributes this to co-tenant interference, an explanation not independently established (S4). All three primary results use the same environment. Quality and timing use different batch sizes and inputs, as detailed in the experimental setting and S4.

\textbf{Across model families.} Table~\ref{tab:cross-model-tpot} extends the steady-state comparison to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-32B on H20-3e, with batch one and 128 generated tokens. Dense-eager and sparse-Graph means differ little at nominal 32K; their ratio grows to 1.96 for Llama at 128K and 1.34 for Qwen3-32B at 64K. This is a comparison of implementations, not an isolated effect of compact scoring. Qwen3-32B also completes 128K decoding in a separately configured run, with a ratio of 1.84; this row is not part of the same-protocol length sweep. These records extend execution evidence across families and model sizes, but provide no matched task-quality or complete-request comparison (S4).



\textbf{Limits of the evidence.} The quality and execution records describe the selected strategy under their respective protocols. They neither isolate the effects of coordinate width, regional scoring or pooled allocation nor establish a matched quality--cost frontier. In particular, Graph/eager token agreement on timing prompts does not establish quality equivalence to dense attention. Ordinary L2 still scores full history, and representation preparation, maintenance and cache setup remain necessary costs. Far gating is disabled; no claim is made for a new far-skipping or KV-release implementation.

\section{Conclusion}
LoDA-SA makes candidate admission and final token competition explicit within a shared low-frequency page-to-token selector. The currently selected pooled configuration provides direct descriptive RULER quality evidence, including heterogeneous task gains and losses. Regional scorer specialization remains unestablished. On the primary H20-3e environment, its Graph implementations record lower steady-state TPOT than dense-eager baselines under different execution regimes. Matched quality and preparation-inclusive complete-request execution remain necessary to establish a quality--cost benefit.
