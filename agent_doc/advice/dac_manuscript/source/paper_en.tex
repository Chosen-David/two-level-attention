\begin{abstract}
Sparse attention must decide which history remains accessible before evaluating attention on a bounded token set. We study the trade-off between candidate access and selection cost at a fixed final token budget. Our near--far cascade combines two mechanisms: a shared 32-coordinate RoPE scoring map for page screening and token refinement, and separate regional page quotas. Final attention uses original-dimensional queries, keys and values. On five Qwen3-8B LongBench tasks, an archived 128/128-protection configuration scores 47.588, compared with 47.910 and 47.666 for nominal full-dimensional page- and token-scoring variants. These descriptive comparisons do not isolate scoring dimensionality or validate the current compact 4/128-protection candidate. In a separate synthetic study, the shared-subspace selector has no lower reported time than the nominal full-dimensional page-scoring variant; historical synchronized TPOT is also higher than dense attention. We therefore present a design and preliminary evaluation, not an established quality--latency improvement. The evidence identifies what a matched evaluation must resolve: whether regional access and compact scoring retain useful tokens at a preparation-inclusive cost below the attention work they remove.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
sparse attention, shared representation, regional retrieval, long-context inference
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Long-context sparse attention replaces full-history attention with attention over selected tokens. Selection itself, however, requires scoring, ranking and memory access. The central design question is therefore how to preserve access to useful history at a fixed final token budget without spending the saved attention cost on retrieval. A page-to-token cascade makes this tension explicit: page screening reduces the candidate support for refinement, but a useful token cannot be recovered once its page is excluded.

Figure~\ref{fig:observation} connects these design choices to four Qwen3-8B diagnostics, retaining the token/page perspective of ClusterKV~\cite{clusterkv}. In the fixed example, mass-ranked, mean-score and extrema-score selection retain different pages (a), motivating a comparison of regional scorers rather than proving a pooling-only effect. Under feasible whole-bin budgets, reserving regional capacity changes distant-history access (b): mean far retention rises from .6759 to .7207, but falls on 43 of 128 correlated observations. Regional allocation is thus a trade-off, not a per-query improvement guarantee. The coordinate diagnostic (c) motivates compact scoring, without isolating frequency from RoPE-pair structure. Historical layer profiles (d) motivate examining selective far computation, but their single-query proxy is not the current gate statistic and provides no decode guarantee. These observations define hypotheses for the cascade; they do not establish production-selector fidelity or task-quality gains.

We examine two coupled decisions within this cascade. The scoring representation determines the information available to rank pages and tokens; regional allocation determines which parts of history can reach refinement. Reducing scoring width does not ensure that distant candidates survive page screening, while reserving distant pages does not ensure inexpensive selection. Our design combines a common low-frequency RoPE coordinate map across both stages with separate near--far page quotas. It retains original-dimensional Q/K/V for attention over the final selected set. The shared map and the regional quotas are the two core mechanisms; a prefill-informed far gate is an optional extension, disabled by default.

This work specifies a shared-map regional cascade and examines three questions: (Q1) what do the available task scores show about compact scoring, (Q2) how do regional budgets constrain candidate access, and (Q3) how do the reported selector and decode costs compare? The evaluation separates historical configuration scores, regional diagnostics and timing measurements rather than combining them into a matched quality--latency result. It reports no established joint advantage: the current compact 4/128-protection candidate lacks matched validation, the synthetic selector time is not lower than the nominal full-dimensional page-scoring variant, and historical synchronized sparse TPOT exceeds dense TPOT. These limits define the status of the design as a research candidate.

\section{Related Work}
Query-aware retrieval methods such as Quest, SparQ and HISA reduce the active attention set through approximate selection~\cite{quest,sparq,hisa}. AsyncTLS provides a page-to-token cascade for sparse selection~\cite{asynctls}. We ask whether a shared low-frequency RoPE scoring view and separate near--far page quotas can retain useful candidates at a fixed final token budget. This separates scoring representation and regional allocation from execution scheduling; their quality and preparation-inclusive cost require matched evaluation.

KV compression and placement methods address complementary memory-capacity and data-movement constraints~\cite{shadowkv,million,clusterkv}. The method studied here preserves original keys and values and changes their selection. Consequently, it does not inherit a reduction in original-KV cache capacity from the compact scoring representation.

\section{Near--Far Selection in a Shared Subspace}
The cascade follows one data path (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). First, a fixed coordinate map supplies the scoring representations for both stages. L1 selects near and far pages under separate quotas; L2 ranks tokens using that same map and the retained page support. The resulting indices are merged with protected positions, and each query recomputes attention from original-dimensional Q/K/V. This separation keeps approximate scoring out of the retained logits, while candidate exclusion remains an approximation. Ordinary L2 still computes full-history scores before masking, so L1 controls access rather than eliminating those dot products.

\subsection{Shared low-frequency coordinates}
For Qwen3-8B, we select post-RoPE coordinates $J=\{48,\ldots,63,112,\ldots,127\}$ from each 128-dimensional query and key. They form 16 complete low-frequency rotation pairs, not a contiguous suffix or a learned projection (Fig.~\ref{fig:detail-subspace}). With coordinate-selection matrix $E_J\in\mathbb R^{128\times32}$, the scoring vectors are $z_q=E_J^\top q$ and $z_i=E_J^\top k_i$. The same map serves both regions and stages within each layer and KV head, while query heads retain their own scores. Slower positional phase variation motivates this choice; it does not guarantee retrieval fidelity. The compact implementation uses width-32 scoring tensors, whereas the historical quality runs used 32 active coordinates in width-128 L2 tensors (S7).

Sharing is defined by coordinates rather than physical storage. For key matrix $\mathbf K$ and regional row selector $R$, $R(\mathbf K E_J)=(R\mathbf K)E_J$: the same coordinate map applies before or after partitioning history. L1 derives page summaries and L2 derives quantize--dequantize token keys from these coordinates; the two need not share a buffer. Original keys and values remain full width, so this design does not reduce original-KV cache capacity. Preparing and maintaining the derived representations remain part of total cost; the algebraic identity alone implies no runtime saving (S7).

\subsection{Regional page and token budgets}
During decoding, let $t$ be the current token's zero-based position in the full sequence, including prefill tokens, so that $S=t+1$ counts valid cached positions, including the current position and excluding padding. Let $s$ and $w$ denote the protected prefix and recent-window lengths. Their union and the unprotected middle are
\begin{align}
\mathcal P_t&=\{i:0\le i\le t,\ i<s\ \text{or}\ i>t-w\},\\
\mathcal M_t&=\{0,\ldots,t\}\setminus\mathcal P_t.
\end{align}
Overlapping protection counts once. Near is a suffix of the unprotected middle history; far is its complement. Near is therefore distinct from the protected sliding window. The near boundary follows fraction $\alpha$ of unprotected history under minimum-page and alignment rules. The unprotected middle has length $\max(0,S-|\mathcal P_t|)$; the selected configuration is specified in the experimental setting.

Figure~\ref{fig:detail-near_far} details the regional cascade. Near uses average-key page scores. Far uses sign-aware extrema in the shared space. For each nonempty region-intersected page $b$, define $m_{b,j}=\min_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$ and $M_{b,j}=\max_{i\in b}z_{i,j}$. Quest's extrema construction~\cite{quest} gives
\begin{equation}
U_b(z_q)=\sum_j\bigl(z_{q,j}^{+}M_{b,j}+z_{q,j}^{-}m_{b,j}\bigr),
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $z^+=\max(z,0)$ and $z^-=\min(z,0)$. This bounds unquantized shared-space dot products, not original-dimensional logits or separately quantized scores. Extrema can come from different tokens; a top-page shortlist can still miss important tokens. Average-key scores do not inherit this bound.

With $K_1=128$ and $\beta=.125$, near and far receive 16 and 112 L1 pages. Regional reservation allocates access, not a quality guarantee: a far page can displace a useful near page. A later token quota cannot recover a token after L1 discarded its page. This distinguishes regional L1 screening from splitting one global shortlist after selection.

For each KV head $h$, the intended regional support consists of valid tokens in retained pages. The example below assumes this support can fill each quota. For the supported budget domain $K\geq|\mathcal P_t|$, the middle cap is $K_{\mathrm{mid}}=K-|\mathcal P_t|$. In the sufficient-length configuration derived from the implementation, a per-head total cap $K=1024$ leaves 892 nominal middle-token slots (Supplementary Fig.~S2). In the standard configuration with sufficient eligible candidates, $\gamma=.625$ acts on near-page capacity, yielding $16\cdot64\cdot.625=640$ near tokens and 252 far slots. The selected configuration protects four prefix and 128 recent tokens. Because four prefix tokens occupy only part of a page, far selection can reselect up to four protected positions; 252 is a nominal quota, not a guaranteed count of unprotected far tokens. Scarce-support and zero-tie behavior are detailed in Supplementary Note~S7.

\subsection{GQA selection and original-dimensional attention}
With grouped-query attention (GQA), 32 query heads and 8 KV heads give four query heads per KV group. L1 averages their page scores before regional TopK. Ordinary L2 scores the full history for each query, applies the shared L1 mask, and averages the independently normalized query probabilities within each KV group. In this Qwen3 implementation, L2 logits retain the original-head scale $1/\sqrt{128}$ before per-query softmax, including when scoring uses 32 coordinates. This probability aggregation differs from applying softmax to averaged logits. Sharing coordinates therefore preserves query-specific distributions, while full-history scoring means that a smaller page shortlist does not itself reduce L2 dot-product work.

\begin{samepage}
The group's unique selected set $\mathcal S_{t,h}$ combines protection and regional winners under the total cap. All queries $g$ in that group use these indices, but recompute their own full-dimensional attention, with head width $d=128$:
\begin{equation}
o_{h,g}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}\frac{e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{i,h}/\sqrt d}}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal S_{t,h}}e^{q_{h,g}^{\top}k_{j,h}/\sqrt d}}v_{i,h}.
\end{equation}
\end{samepage}
Merging token indices before this softmax avoids adding separately normalized regional outputs~\cite{salo}. Retained logits use original KV, but restricting the denominator can change attention output. Selected token indices define a common original-space attention set. Physical gathering and reuse depend on the attention backend and sequence length; the inspected implementation also has a short-sequence dense fallback. 

\subsection{Prefill-informed far gating}
The optional prefill-informed gate (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) asks whether prefill observations can identify layers where repeated far selection can be omitted. Per input and layer, mean prefill far-attention weight sum below $\epsilon$ sets OFF; otherwise far selection stays active. The decision freezes at decode entry and is reused. The feature is disabled by default. In the supported fixed-quota implementation, OFF skips both far scoring stages and their selections, while unused L2 quota refills near candidates without expanding the near-page budget. Representation preparation and sequence-wide score buffers and normalization remain.

Figure~\ref{fig:detail-gate} shows the gate decision and refill. We implement this extension for a single sequence; Supplementary Note~S5 gives the observation boundary and state transitions. Its threshold controls a prefill statistic rather than subsequent attention error. The gate is therefore an approximation policy whose effect on quality and total cost must be evaluated separately from the default ungated method.

\section{Evaluation and Discussion}
\subsection{Q1: What do archived scoring configurations show?}
\textbf{Descriptive quality comparison.} Across HotpotQA, Qasper, MuSiQue, GovReport and RepoBench-P~\cite{longbench}, the mean scores are 47.588 (shared-subspace configuration), 47.910 (nominal full-dimensional page-scoring variant) and 47.666 (nominal full-dimensional token-scoring variant). Relative to the shared-subspace configuration, the nominal page- and token-scoring variants are higher by 0.322 and 0.078 points; the latter is 0.244 points below the former. Neither variant is verified as a controlled change in scoring dimensionality. Their score differences are therefore descriptive and do not isolate either stage's effect (Supplementary Fig.~S4 and Note~S7). The archived shared-subspace configuration score does not directly validate the current compact implementation. None is dense FullKV.

\subsection{Q2: How do regional budgets constrain candidate access?}
The regional diagnostic in Figure~\ref{fig:observation} motivates reserved capacity, but the available task scores do not isolate its benefit. The current compact candidate lacks a matched dense FullKV comparison. Figure~\ref{fig:fullkv-quality} preserves the historical 128K scores. Applying the inspected quota formula to the reported $\beta=.375$, $K_1=128$, page size 64, $\gamma=.625$ and 128/128 protection gives a nominal far-token quota of zero within the 768-token middle budget. Thus reserving far pages does not by itself guarantee a nonzero final far-token quota. This code/configuration inference does not verify the executed path or isolate the cause of the score gap.

The protection-only configuration scores 26.09 against a 47.91 reference, while using 256 rather than 1024 tokens. The difference therefore combines retrieval-policy and budget effects.

\subsection{Q3: How do reported selection and decode costs compare?}
\textbf{Model-level decode diagnostic.} Using dense attention at each input length as the reference, Figure~\ref{fig:pending-evaluation} compares author-reported dynamic-cache TPOT for the archived sparse configuration. At 32K, 64K and 128K, sparse medians are 87.9, 97.6 and 119.9 ms, versus 26.0, 39.7 and 67.3 ms for dense attention: sparse-to-dense ratios of reported medians of 3.38, 2.46 and 1.78. Across these three reported lengths, the ratio decreases while sparse TPOT rises and remains above dense TPOT. This synchronized diagnostic excludes tokenization, input transfer and text output; it is not complete-request latency. These aggregate medians do not identify a causal bottleneck; they are not matched quality--cost measurements of the compact 4/128 candidate. Repetition and execution-path limitations are detailed in S6.

\textbf{Local selector cost.} A separate synthetic experiment uses single-layer, batch-one, single-query QKV at length 64K. Its steady-state timing summaries from two rounds are 1.3502 ms for nominal full-dimensional page scoring, 1.3598 ms for shared-subspace scoring and 1.4634 ms for nominal full-dimensional token scoring. The shared-subspace selector thus has no lower reported time than the nominal page-scoring variant in this setting. This ordering does not identify a limiting stage, and the unresolved sampling-protocol discrepancy prevents a reliable interpretation of small timing differences (Supplementary Note~S6). The operator timings are not paired with the LongBench quality runs and do not measure complete-request cost.

\subsection{Optional gate: prefill is not a decode guarantee} Prefill statistics also fail to bound later far-region demand: an observed input--layer pair with prefill far-attention weight sum below .05 reaches .1678 during decoding. Relative to the reported reference score of 47.910, gate thresholds of .05 and .10 are associated with mean decreases of .034 and .218 points, respectively; three of five tasks decline in each configuration, including HotpotQA decreases of 1.05 and 1.26 points. The larger per-task losses demonstrate that a small average change can conceal task sensitivity.

The complete scoring configuration is not identified in these gate summaries, so the results cannot be attributed to the gated shared-subspace method (Supplementary Note~S6).

\textbf{Implications.} Shared coordinates reduce scoring dimensionality, and regional quotas reserve candidate access; neither establishes a quality--cost advantage. Historical scores do not isolate stage effects, regional comparisons do not establish allocation benefits, and separate timing records cannot establish a matched gain. The optional gate adds approximation and task sensitivity. Non-inferiority and complete-request acceleration therefore require matched evaluation of the compact candidate.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a near--far cascade combining shared low-frequency RoPE scoring and regional page quotas, with original-dimensional final attention. The trade-off is candidate access versus selection cost at a fixed token budget. Historical Qwen3 evidence does not validate the compact candidate's joint quality and cost: synthetic selector timing does not improve on nominal full-dimensional page scoring, sparse TPOT exceeds dense, and prefill statistics do not guarantee decode behavior. The design requires matched, preparation-inclusive evaluation.
